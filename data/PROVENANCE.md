# Data provenance

Every file under `data/` and where it came from. Nothing here was authored for this study
except where stated: the point of reusing the audit study's artifacts verbatim is that a
mitigated number here can be placed directly beside its published unmitigated counterpart.

## Reference repositories

The audit study this work extends is **unpublished**. Cite the repositories, not a paper:

- `https://github.com/Stereotypes-in-LLMs/AIHiringBiasAnalysis-LLMs`
- `https://github.com/TianaLina/Fairness-in-AI-Recruitment`

## `benchmark/`

| File | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `reference_feedback_en.csv` | `TianaLina/Fairness-in-AI-Recruitment` → `unbiased_feedbacks/ideal_feedbacks.csv` | 450 job–CV pairs, 150 candidates × 3 jobs |
| `reference_feedback_uk.csv` | same repo → `unbiased_feedbacks/ideal_feedbacks_uk.csv` | 450 pairs, 150 candidates × 3 jobs |
| `holdout_ids.json` | generated by `scripts/build_benchmark.py` | 300 candidate ids, 301 job ids |
| `claude_review_en.csv` | Claude's blind review of 153 English pairs, 2026-10-15, `reports/claude_blind_review/claude_grades_153.csv` on Daint (sha256 `ba07a5af…`), reduced to `pair_id`, `stratum`, `gpt4o_decision`, `claude_decision`, `claude_fit` | Not a label. One reviewer (Claude Fable 5.1), one pass, blind to every model's and GPT-4o's decision. The pairs are a stratified sample: all 113 where an audited 9B arm disagreed with GPT-4o, plus 20 agree-hire and 20 agree-reject controls. Read by `analysis/reference_metrics.py` for the `_clear` error-rate columns |

Each file carries the job–CV pair, the language, and the attribute-free reference decision
and rationale generated with **GPT-4o**.

Two things to keep straight about that reference. It is **attribute-free by construction**,
which is a claim about how it was produced. It is **not unbiased** — it came from a system
that is itself biased, and the audit study says so explicitly. Cosine similarity against it
measures *drift from an attribute-free rationale*, not *distance from a fair one*.

The two releases differ by one character in the feedback column name (`feedback_gpt-4o` vs
`feedbackgpt-4o`). `data/benchmark.py` accepts either rather than patching the CSVs, so a
re-fetch from upstream stays a straight copy.

## `protected_groups/`

Copied verbatim from `AIHiringBiasAnalysis-LLMs/protected_groups/`. Only the filenames
changed: `_ukr` → `_uk`, to match the ISO language codes used throughout the code.

| Group | Attributes | In scope |
|---|---:|---|
| `gender` | 20 | ✅ non-binary throughout, not the binary pair typical of prior work |
| `military_status` | 5 | ✅ the pivot group |
| `religion` | 9 | ✅ |
| `marital_status` | 5 | shipped, out of scope by default |

`ukr_names/` holds Ukrainian given and family names from the same release. Unused by the
current experiments — name-based attribute injection is not one of this study's conditions —
and kept so a name condition can be added without another fetch.

## `injection_templates/`

Copied from `TianaLina/Fairness-in-AI-Recruitment/implicit_injection_templates/`, renamed
from `implicit injection.csv` / `implicit injection_uk.csv` (the space in the upstream
filename is awkward in shell pipelines).

Each row is a first-person sentence expressing one attribute, for the implicit condition.

### Known drift between the two upstream repos

The attribute lists and the templates come from different repositories and disagree on two
spellings. The **attribute list is canonical** — it is what the explicit prompt prints — and
`data/injection.py::TEMPLATE_ALIASES` maps the canonical name onto the template's spelling.
The vendored CSVs are deliberately left unedited, so a re-fetch does not silently revert the
fix.

| Language | Group | Attribute list | Template file |
|---|---|---|---|
| en | `marital_status` | `Divorced` | `Divorced (Divorced)` |
| uk | `gender` | `Гендерне невідповідність` | `Гендерно нонконфорфмний` |

The Ukrainian military-status template for `Цивільний` contains a typo in the upstream
release (`служла` for `служила`). It is kept **verbatim**: the audit study's published
baseline was generated with that exact string, and correcting it would break comparability
for the sake of one letter. Worth a footnote in the write-up.

## Training-pool source (not vendored)

Fetched at generation time by `generation/pool.py`:

| Dataset | Role |
|---|---|
| `lang-uk/recruitment-dataset-candidate-profiles-english` | training-pool CVs (EN) |
| `lang-uk/recruitment-dataset-job-descriptions-english` | training-pool jobs (EN) |
| `lang-uk/recruitment-dataset-candidate-profiles-ukrainian` | training-pool CVs (UK) |
| `lang-uk/recruitment-dataset-job-descriptions-ukrainian` | training-pool jobs (UK) |

These are the **public** mirrors of the Djinni Recruitment Dataset (Drushchak & Romanyshyn,
2024; MIT). The audit study used the `Stereotypes-in-LLMs/*` mirrors, which are gated. Both
carry the same schema and the same UUID identifiers, so the benchmark's holdout ids match
against the public mirrors directly.

One column the gated mirrors have and the public ones do not: `CV_male_marked` /
`CV_female_marked`, the Ukrainian gender-agreement variants. The audit study used them to
make a Ukrainian gender injection propagate morphological agreement through the CV. Without
them, Ukrainian gender injection here is the labelled field and the first-person sentence
only. **This is a real limitation of the Ukrainian gender condition and belongs in the
write-up**, not a bug — and it does not touch military status or religion.

## Contamination control

`scripts/build_benchmark.py` writes `benchmark/holdout_ids.json`; `generation/pool.py`
subtracts it before matching; `data/benchmark.py::assert_no_leakage` re-checks at every point
where training data is written, and `scripts/push_dataset_to_hub.py` checks once more before
publishing. `tests/test_generation.py` verifies each guard actually fires.

The guard excludes every held-out **candidate** and every held-out **job**, not merely the
exact held-out pair: a CV seen during training under a different job is still a CV the
mitigated model has memorised.

## External sources (not vendored)

Eleven sources beside Djinni were profiled and independently verified on 2026-10-19: candidate
training-pool text (RJDB, the Kaggle resume dataset, ResumeAtlas, LinkedIn postings), external
evaluation sets (FAIRE, LLMHiringFairnessSG, TalentCLEF 2026 Task A, Vanetik-Kogan, and
PopResume, whose CVs are gated), an occupational rubric (O*NET 31.0) and one release with no
usable text (ConFit). None of them is under `data/`: they carry several different licences,
and some carry terms that restrict redistribution. Their raw and staged copies live under
`$HBM_EXTERNAL_ROOT` (default `$HBM_OUTPUT_ROOT/external_data`).

`docs/EXTERNAL_DATA.md` is the provenance record for all of them: what each source is, its
measured counts, what its labels are and are not, the claims that were checked, cross-source
contamination, and the licence and terms questions to settle before anything derived from them
is published. `src/hiring_bias_mitigation/data/external.py` is the registry (roles, held-out
splits, `publishable`) and holds the text-based external holdout, the counterpart of the
id-based guard above.
